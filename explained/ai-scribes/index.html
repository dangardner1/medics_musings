AI scribes, explained

Your doctor used to type while you talked. Now an app listens, types, and occasionally decides you mentioned a pet iguana. What could possibly get lost in translation?

The short answer

An AI scribe (also called ambient AI documentation, or “the phone on the counter that's listening”) is software that hears your visit through a phone or computer microphone and drafts the visit note: history, exam findings, assessment and plan. Your clinician then reviews and edits the draft before it goes into your medical record. The robot writes the first draft; the human signs the confession.

Why clinics suddenly love them

Documentation is one of the biggest time sinks in medicine; many doctors finish their notes at night, a ritual lovingly called “pajama time.” Early studies and health-system pilots report less time on notes and lower reported burnout, and many patients say their doctor finally looks at them instead of the keyboard. The evidence is still young and varies by product and specialty, but “my doctor made eye contact” is already a medical breakthrough.

What can go wrong

AI scribes can mishear, leave things out, credit a statement to the wrong person, or add details nobody said, a “hallucination,” which is a fancy word for making things up with confidence. That's why your clinician stays responsible for the note and should read it before signing. If your visit summary says something you never said, ask for a correction; you have a right to request amendments to your record, iguana or no iguana.

Privacy, consent and saying no

In the U.S., vendors that handle your health information for a clinic generally work under HIPAA business associate agreements. Clinics should tell you when a visit is being recorded and ask for your consent, and some states require everyone's consent to record. You can say no, and your care shouldn't change if you do. The app will survive the rejection.

Frequently asked questions

Is my doctor's visit being recorded by AI?

It might be. Clinics using AI scribes should tell you and ask for consent. If you're not sure, ask; you're allowed to decline, and no, it won't hurt the software's feelings.

Who checks the note an AI scribe writes?

Your clinician. The AI writes a draft, and the clinician is responsible for reviewing, correcting and signing it.

Do AI scribes make mistakes?

Yes. They can leave things out, credit the wrong person or invent details. Read your visit summary in your patient portal and ask for corrections if something is wrong.
