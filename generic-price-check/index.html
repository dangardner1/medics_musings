Generic Price Check

Every week, Medicaid surveys what retail pharmacies actually pay for drugs. Here is what a basket of the most common generics really costs the pharmacy, next to what a satirical bill says it costs. The pharmacy costs are real. The bills are not.

This week 11 of 22 generics got cheaper for the pharmacy and 11 got more expensive. The biggest mover: Metoprolol succinate ER 25 mg, up 11.1%. The cheapest pill on the list is Amlodipine 5 mg, at $0.0102 a tablet: about a penny, with change. One typical supply of all 22 costs the pharmacy $31.62. Our satirical bill for the same basket comes to $18,514.70. Nobody can explain the difference, which is how you know it’s working.

About the numbers

Real: “Pharmacy pays” is the National Average Drug Acquisition Cost (NADAC), Medicaid’s weekly survey of what retail pharmacies pay wholesalers and manufacturers for a drug. Figures are from NADAC (National Average Drug Acquisition Cost) 2026, published by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, via data.medicaid.gov, as of Oct 21, 2026. A month’s supply here assumes a typical dose; the per-tablet price is CMS’s. NADAC leaves out the pharmacy’s dispensing costs, and what you pay depends on your plan, your pharmacy and your deductible. How NADAC works.

Satire: the “billed as” figures, the statement of charges and the commentary are made up. They are not prices, bills or charges from any pharmacy, insurer or pharmacy benefit manager, and they are not financial or medical advice. The jokes are about the pricing system, never about anyone who takes these medicines.
