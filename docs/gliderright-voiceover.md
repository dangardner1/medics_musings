# Have You Oiled Your Balls Today? An AI-voice infomercial

A satirical voiceover to play with **The GlideRight Playbook**, the 11-slide NotebookLM deck
(https://notebooklm.link.google/mLbAl6xx9dql). About 4 minutes, one block per slide, timed to
the deck. It also works as a standalone audio piece.

GlideRight is a fictional walker-ball lubricant. The jokes are aimed at infomercials, investor
pitches and health-care pricing, never at people who use walkers, canes or shower chairs. The
script says out loud that it is satire.

## Voices

| Role | What it sounds like | Suggested ElevenLabs voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ANNOUNCER** | Bright, breathless infomercial host who has never been tired | Jamie, Upbeat & Professional (`Ep4Gg4tSVvRRsoajLwJY`) |
| **FINE PRINT** | Calm, flat and fast, like the legal read at the end of a drug ad | Tom, Calm Advertising (`VDYfIKeHJwXUpUQhs8FR`), the narrator of the site explainer; sped up in the edit |

Generate each block as its own clip (as we did for the site explainer), then lay them over the
slides. Tags in [brackets] are delivery directions: use them with `eleven_v3`, or drop them
with `eleven_multilingual_v2`.

## Script

**[SLIDE 1: "Have You Oiled Your Balls Today?"]**

ANNOUNCER: [big, bright] Have you oiled your balls today? [beat] Relax. We mean the tennis
balls on the bottom of the walker. Please get your mind out of the clinic. This is the
GlideRight Playbook.

**[SLIDE 2: "Anatomy of a Bad Walk"]**

ANNOUNCER: Every bad walk has a body. Increased physical strain. Embarrassing drag noise.
And inconsistent surface wear, which is what we call the floor's opinion. Together they make
one sound. [SFX: a long, sad squeak] That one.

**[SLIDE 3: "The Friction Factor: A Microscopic View"]**

ANNOUNCER: Under a microscope, the dry ball grabs, sticks and drags. The GlideRight ball?
Effortless glide. Our proprietary, medical-safe Dry-Glide Film cuts the coefficient of
friction by up to sixty percent.

FINE PRINT: [fast] Measured in our study, which was a hallway, and us.

**[SLIDE 4: "The Medical Breakthrough: Walker Ball Lubrication Therapy"]**

ANNOUNCER: This is not a household hack. This is Walker Ball Lubrication Therapy. W. B. L. T.
Yes, like the sandwich, but for feet. Targeted precision. Clinical safety: non-toxic,
non-staining. Lifespan extension: two to three times longer. And universal application:
hardwood, laminate, tile, vinyl, even low-pile carpet.

**[SLIDE 5: "Smooth Gliding in Under a Minute"]**

ANNOUNCER: Three steps. Under a minute. One: wipe. Two: drop. Three: glide. Wipe each ball.
Squeeze a single drop. Wait sixty seconds. [beat] A sixty-second procedure, with no prior
authorization required. In health care, we call that a miracle.

**[SLIDE 6: "The Lubrication Showdown"]**

ANNOUNCER: Some people reach for W-D-forty, or cooking spray. Greasy mess. Stained floor.
Gone in a day. Cooking spray belongs on a pan. Your walker is not a pan. [beat] Yet.

**[SLIDE 7: "Decibels of Squeak / The Swagger Index"]**

ANNOUNCER: The results! A forty-seven percent reduction in hallway squeak. And a sixty-three
percent increase in unsolicited compliments, posture, confidence and finger guns.

FINE PRINT: [fast] Swagger is not a validated outcome measure. Over-lubrication may lead to
excessive glide syndrome and accidental hallway laps of honor.

**[SLIDE 8: "Real Life, Smoother Steps"]**

ANNOUNCER: Don't take our word for it. [warmly] "Mom's walker used to screech down the
hallway at six a.m. Now the house is finally quiet." Rebecca K., daughter and caregiver.
"Genuinely less effort to walk." James M., verified buyer. "A no-brainer." Diane P., physical
therapist. Currently rolling in twelve assisted living facilities, with a seventy percent
reorder rate.

FINE PRINT: [deadpan] The only rate in health care that high that is not a readmission.
Testimonials are fictional. So is Diane.

**[SLIDE 9: "Good to Know"]**

ANNOUNCER: Good to know! Will it stain floors? No. Tennis-ball feet? Yes. How often do you
reapply? Every two to four weeks. One little bottle holds two hundred drops. And canes and
shower chairs? Absolutely. Anything with a rubber or felt foot.

**[SLIDE 10: "Pick Your Glide"]**

ANNOUNCER: Pick your glide! The single bottle, fourteen ninety-five. The twin pack, most
popular, twenty-four ninety-five. The family three-pack, thirty-two ninety-five. Free
shipping. And a thirty-day guarantee: don't love it, we refund you, and you keep the bottle.

FINE PRINT: [flat] Which is more than your health plan offers. Your plan will cover none of
this and will still send an explanation of benefits.

**[SLIDE 11: "The Frictionless Formula"]**

ANNOUNCER: The frictionless formula. Mechanical: up to sixty percent less friction and zero
noise. Practical: a one-minute, mess-free protocol. Emotional: restored dignity, quiet homes
and maximum swagger. [slower, warm] Silence the squeak. Restore the glide. Welcome to
frictionless independence.

**[END CARD]**

FINE PRINT: [very fast] GlideRight is fictional, and so are its investors. Any resemblance to
a real product, an investor letter or a walker is coincidental. Side effects of this program
may include laughing in hallways, an urge to check your walker, and unrealistic expectations
of the oil industry. This is satire, not medical or investment advice. A Medics Musings
production. No prior authorization required.

## Timing and sound

- About 530 spoken words. As produced it runs 4:07 (multilingual v2 voices, a beat after each slide,
  and the fine print sped up 10 to 22 percent for the legal-read effect).
- Sound cues (synthesized in the edit): a falling squeak after slide 2's setup line; a soft "ding" on
  the price reveal in slide 10; a quiet hold-music arpeggio under the slide 10 fine print.
  Everything else stays dry.
- Keep the slide 8 testimonials warm and sincere. The joke is the marketing around them.
