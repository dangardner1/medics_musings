Teaching guide · Dignity, shame and technology in caring for older adults

The Robot Who Wipes My Butt

For
Geriatrics, internal medicine and family medicine trainees; nursing students; long-term care staff
Episode
4 min
Session
30–45 minutes

Episode page & transcript

Listen: https://www.medicsmusings.com/episodes/the-robot-who-wipes-my-butt/

Learning objectives

After listening and discussing, participants should be able to:

  1. Discuss how intimate care affects the dignity of older adults and their relationships with family and aides.
  2. Evaluate potential benefits and harms of care robots in long-term care.
  3. Identify the parts of care that technology should not replace.

Discussion questions

  1. The narrator says shame doesn't transfer to a machine. Is that a good thing? When might it be a problem?
  2. Gerald asks for consent before every step. How consistently do human caregivers do the same?
  3. “A robot that replaces your Sunday visit is an abandonment with good UX.” Where is the line between support and replacement?
  4. How should consent work for care technology when a resident has cognitive impairment?
  5. What would you want a care robot to do for your own parent, and what would you never want it to do?

Quick activity

Rewrite the robot's consent script for a resident with moderate dementia. What changes?

How to run it

Play the episode together (4 minutes) or assign it beforehand. Start with the first question to let people react to the humor, then move to the harder ones. Remind the group that the episode is satire and some of its “facts” are invented; spotting which ones is a good exercise in itself.
